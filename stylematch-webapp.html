Onboarding flow

Keep it short, but make every question highly diagnostic.

Step 01
Micro-style questionnaire

Ask 8 to 10 forced-choice questions that reveal comfort zone, visual drama tolerance, fit preference, shopping intent, and accessory confidence without making the user feel like they are filling out a survey.

Step 02
Face analysis capture

After taste is known, request one clean front-facing photo and one optional profile shot. The app explains why: better color harmony, eyewear, earring, neck line, and silhouette recommendations.

Step 03
Precision style graph

The engine scores complexion undertone, eye contrast, face geometry, hair texture impression, piercings, and visual edge level, then merges those signals with the style questionnaire.

Step 04
Personalized storefront

Once logged in, the same motion-heavy interface re-skins itself to the user’s own palette, energy, and preferred clothing drama while surfacing dropship products from partner stores.

Precise questions

These are short, sharp, and specific enough to guide the model before any photo analysis starts.

Question set A
What the user is trying to communicate
  • When you enter a room, would you rather look polished, expressive, intimidating, relaxed, or quietly expensive?
  • Pick the tension you enjoy most: clean vs layered, fitted vs loose, subtle vs flashy, soft vs sharp.
  • How much visual attention do you want your clothes to demand on an average day?
  • Which details feel most like you: metal jewelry, texture, color, structure, logos, or none?
Question set B
What the user will realistically wear
  • Which silhouettes make you feel best: close to body, balanced, oversized, long line, cropped, or mixed?
  • What bothers you most when clothes miss: wrong color, wrong fit, too loud, too plain, too trendy, or bad quality feel?
  • How often are you shopping for work, everyday life, going out, content creation, travel, or special events?
  • How open are you to trying a version of your style that is 15% bolder than what you wear now?

Recommended product architecture

Questionnaire layer

Creates a style intent vector before any visual inference happens.

Computer vision layer

Reads non-sensitive appearance attributes and returns confidence values rather than hard labels.

Styling rules + retrieval

Maps user vectors to tagged catalog items and outfit bundles.

Personal theme engine

Re-skins gradients, cards, highlights, and feed art from the user profile when they log in.
